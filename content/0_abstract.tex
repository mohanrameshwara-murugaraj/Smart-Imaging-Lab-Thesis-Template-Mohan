\chapter*{Abstract\markboth{Abstract}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Fetal diffusion magnetic resonance imaging (dMRI) is particularly susceptible to fetal motion and acquisition-related artifacts, which can compromise subsequent reconstruction and quantitative analysis. Reliable automated quality control is therefore important for identifying corrupted image dynamics and reducing the amount of unsuitable data entering downstream processing. This thesis investigates artificial intelligence-based quality control for fetal dMRI through two complementary approaches: a volumetric 3D pipeline designed to preserve spatial context and a lightweight 2D slice-wise pipeline designed to reduce computational processing requirements.

The 3D approach combines fetal-brain localization using nnU-Net with brain-focused cropping, intensity normalization, and artifact classification using a residual 3D convolutional neural network. In a matched evaluation comprising 71 MRI dynamics, including 12 artifact-labelled dynamics, the 3D pipeline detected all artifact cases, achieving 100\% artifact recall with no false negatives. However, the complete volumetric processing pipeline required approximately 25 seconds per dynamic, with much of the computational cost associated with upstream 3D brain localization and preprocessing.

To investigate a computationally lighter alternative, a 2D pipeline was developed in which selected slices were independently processed using a MobileNetV3-U-Net for fetal-brain localization followed by brain-focused cropping and EfficientNet-B0-based artifact classification. Slice-level predictions were subsequently combined to obtain a dynamic-level quality decision. The complete successful-slice processing path required approximately 5.24 ms per slice. In the initial matched comparison, the 2D approach detected 6 of 12 artifact dynamics, corresponding to 50\% recall. Further investigation of slice-to-dynamic aggregation and decision thresholds showed that artifact sensitivity could be substantially improved; a Top-k aggregation strategy achieved 83.3\% recall while retaining high specificity. These experiments demonstrate that the final quality-control performance of the slice-wise approach depends strongly on how localized artifact evidence is aggregated across the dynamic.

Overall, the results reveal a clear trade-off between artifact-detection sensitivity and computational efficiency. The 3D pipeline currently provides the more reliable artifact detector by exploiting full volumetric information, whereas the 2D architecture offers a considerably lighter sequential processing strategy with potential for reducing post-acquisition computational time-to-decision. The findings further highlight the importance of robust fetal-brain localization, appropriate slice-to-dynamic aggregation, and reliable annotation strategies when developing automated quality-control systems for fetal dMRI.
