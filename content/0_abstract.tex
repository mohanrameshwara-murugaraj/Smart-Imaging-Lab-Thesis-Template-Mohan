\chapter*{Abstract\markboth{Abstract}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Fetal diffusion magnetic resonance imaging (dMRI) provides valuable information about the developing fetal brain by characterizing the diffusion of water molecules within biological tissues. However, spontaneous fetal movement and acquisition-related artifacts can compromise image quality, affect subsequent diffusion analysis, and require time-consuming repeated acquisitions. Since image-quality assessment is commonly performed after acquisition, automated artifact detection is important for identifying corrupted data and supporting earlier quality-control decisions.

This master's thesis investigates and compares two deep learning-based approaches for automated artifact detection in low-field fetal dMRI. The initial three-dimensional (3D) framework employs nnU-Net v2 for fetal-brain segmentation and a 3D ResNet classifier for identifying artifact-corrupted diffusion dynamics. To investigate quality assessment at the individual-slice level, a two-dimensional (2D) framework was subsequently developed using MobileNetV3-U-Net for brain segmentation and EfficientNet-B0 for artifact classification. Following a segmentation-based quality-control approach, both pipelines extract the fetal-brain region before classification. The models were evaluated using fetal diffusion MRI datasets through segmentation and classification experiments, including comparisons of brain-region extraction, computational performance, and dynamic-level quality decisions. The volumetric approach showed promising artifact-detection performance, while the slice-wise approach enabled lower-latency processing but faced challenges in identifying artifacts from individual slices. Further experiments demonstrated that incorporating segmentation failures into dynamic-level decisions improved artifact detection in the evaluated data. Overall, the findings highlight the trade-off between volumetric information and slice-wise processing and provide a foundation for developing automated quality control during fetal MRI acquisition.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Fetal diffusion MRI, artifact detection, deep learning, fetal-brain segmentation, nnU-Net, 3D ResNet, MobileNetV3-U-Net, EfficientNet-B0, image-quality assessment.
