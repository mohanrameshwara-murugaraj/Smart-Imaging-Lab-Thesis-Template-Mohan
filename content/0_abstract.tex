\chapter*{Abstract\markboth{Abstract}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Fetal diffusion magnetic resonance imaging (dMRI) is susceptible to fetal motion and acquisition-related artifacts that can compromise image quality and subsequent analysis. This thesis investigates automated artifact assessment using deep learning, with particular emphasis on the transition from evaluating complete three-dimensional (3D) diffusion dynamics to processing individual two-dimensional (2D) slices for potentially earlier quality-control decisions.

An initial volumetric pipeline combined fetal-brain localization using nnU-Net with 3D ResNet artifact classification. A subsequent slice-wise pipeline used MobileNetV3-U-Net to segment the fetal brain, extracted brain-centered regions of interest, and applied EfficientNet-B0 for artifact classification. Oracle-mask and predicted-mask crops were evaluated to examine the influence of automatic localization. Slice-level predictions were also combined into dynamic-level decisions, including rules that accounted for unsuccessful brain-crop generation.

On a held-out 2D segmentation test set, MobileNetV3-U-Net achieved a Dice coefficient of 0.8620. On 1,499 matched classifier-ready test slices, oracle-crop and predicted-crop classification yielded receiver operating characteristic area-under-the-curve values of 0.6440 and 0.6335, respectively, against slice labels derived from dynamic-level annotations. On a separate evaluation of 88 dynamics, a validation-selected failure-aware decision rule achieved 66.7\% artifact sensitivity and 94.3\% specificity, compared with 16.7\% sensitivity and 94.3\% specificity for the original 2D decision rule. Batch-size-one segmentation inference took approximately 2.75\,ms per slice on the evaluated GPU. These computational measurements do not establish end-to-end scanner-integrated real-time performance.

Recorded 3D predictions showed promising artifact detection in the examined cohorts; however, the independence of the 3D evaluation data from model development could not be fully established, limiting direct generalization claims. Overall, the experiments demonstrate the feasibility of constructing volumetric and slice-wise artifact-assessment workflows while identifying important limitations arising from weak slice-level labels, unreliable localization on artifact-corrupted images, and decision-threshold selection. Further subject-independent validation and prospective acquisition-time evaluation are required before clinical deployment can be assessed.
