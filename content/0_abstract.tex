\chapter*{Abstract\markboth{Abstract}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Fetal diffusion magnetic resonance imaging (dMRI) provides valuable information about the developing fetal brain by characterizing water diffusion within biological tissues. However, spontaneous fetal movement, maternal motion, low signal-to-noise ratio, and acquisition-related artifacts can compromise image quality and affect subsequent reconstruction and quantitative analysis. Since corrupted acquisitions may require time-consuming repetitions, automated quality assessment is important for identifying artifacts and enabling earlier quality feedback.

This master's thesis investigates two deep learning-based pipelines for automated artifact detection in low-field fetal dMRI. The initial three-dimensional (3D) approach combines nnU-Net v2 for fetal-brain segmentation with a 3D ResNet classifier to assess the image quality of complete diffusion dynamics. To investigate earlier artifact detection, a two-dimensional (2D) approach was developed using MobileNetV3-U-Net for fetal-brain segmentation and EfficientNet-B0 for slice-level artifact classification. Both pipelines were evaluated in terms of segmentation, artifact classification, and computational performance, while additional experiments investigated brain-region extraction, prediction aggregation, and sequential quality assessment.

The 3D pipeline demonstrated promising artifact-detection performance using volumetric information, although complete-volume processing restricted opportunities for earlier quality decisions. The 2D pipeline achieved strong overall brain-segmentation performance and low inference latency, but classification was affected by limited spatial context, segmentation failures, and weak slice-level annotations. Incorporating segmentation failures into dynamic-level decisions improved artifact sensitivity in the evaluated data.

Overall, this study highlights the strengths and limitations of volumetric and slice-wise artifact detection in fetal dMRI. The findings provide a foundation for developing automated acquisition-time quality control, while further independent validation and real-time scanner integration remain necessary.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Fetal diffusion MRI, artifact detection, deep learning, fetal-brain segmentation, nnU-Net, 3D ResNet, MobileNetV3-U-Net, EfficientNet-B0, image-quality assessment.
