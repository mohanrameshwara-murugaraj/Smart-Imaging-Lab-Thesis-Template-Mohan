\chapter*{Abstract\markboth{Abstract}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Fetal diffusion magnetic resonance imaging (dMRI) provides valuable information about the developing fetal brain by characterizing water diffusion within biological tissues, enabling the investigation of brain microstructure beyond conventional anatomical imaging. However, fetal dMRI is particularly susceptible to spontaneous fetal movement, maternal motion, low signal-to-noise ratio, and acquisition-related artifacts. These disturbances can compromise image quality, affect subsequent reconstruction and quantitative analysis, and potentially require time-consuming repeated acquisitions. Since image-quality assessment is commonly performed after data acquisition, corrupted images may not be identified early enough for immediate reacquisition. Automated quality-control methods are therefore important for improving the reliability and efficiency of fetal dMRI examinations.

This master's thesis investigates the development and evaluation of deep learning-based approaches for automated artifact detection in low-field fetal dMRI. Two processing frameworks are examined to assess the relationship between artifact-detection performance, spatial information, and computational requirements. The initial three-dimensional (3D) pipeline employs nnU-Net v2 for fetal-brain segmentation, followed by brain-region extraction and a 3D ResNet classifier to distinguish artifact-corrupted from good-quality diffusion dynamics. A subsequent two-dimensional (2D) pipeline combines MobileNetV3-U-Net for fetal-brain localization with EfficientNet-B0 for slice-level artifact classification, aiming to enable quality assessment before completion of an entire dynamic. Both approaches are evaluated through segmentation and classification experiments, while additional investigations examine brain-region extraction, classification errors, computational performance, and dynamic-level decision strategies.

The volumetric approach demonstrated promising artifact-detection performance in the recorded experiments, although its assessment of complete dynamics limits opportunities for earlier quality feedback. The slice-wise framework achieved strong overall fetal-brain segmentation performance and low computational inference latency, but artifact classification remained challenging due to limited spatial context, unreliable localization in artifact-corrupted images, and the use of dynamic-level annotations as weak slice-level labels. Incorporating segmentation failures into dynamic-level decision strategies improved artifact sensitivity in the evaluated data. The experiments also identified limitations in the available validation evidence, preventing definitive conclusions about comparative generalization and real-time clinical performance.

Overall, this work provides an experimental investigation of volumetric and slice-wise deep learning approaches for automated fetal dMRI artifact detection. The findings highlight the importance of spatial context, reliable brain localization, and suitable quality-decision strategies, providing a foundation for further development of acquisition-time quality control in fetal MRI.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Fetal diffusion MRI, image artifacts, fetal-brain segmentation, deep learning, nnU-Net, 3D ResNet, MobileNetV3-U-Net, EfficientNet-B0, image-quality assessment, real-time quality control.
